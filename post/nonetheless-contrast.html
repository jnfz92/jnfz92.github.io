Nonetheless: Formal Contrast in English

Learn how nonetheless connects contrasting ideas and how to punctuate it.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B2–C1

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

Nonetheless means nevertheless or even so. It introduces a statement that remains true despite the information just mentioned.

At the beginning of a sentence it is normally followed by a comma. It can also follow a semicolon when connecting two closely related independent clauses.

2. Bilingual examples

The task was difficult. Nonetheless, we completed it on time.
La tarea era difícil. No obstante, la terminamos a tiempo.
The schedule was demanding; nonetheless, everyone completed the course.
El horario era exigente; no obstante, todos completaron el curso.
The evidence was limited. Nonetheless, the team continued the study.
Las pruebas eran limitadas. No obstante, el equipo continuó el estudio.

3. Rules and common mistakes

Nonetheless is relatively formal. In everyday conversation, but, still, or even so may sound more natural.

Do not add formal connectors merely to sound advanced. Use nonetheless when the contrast between the two ideas is genuinely clear.

Study tip: Say the English example aloud before checking the Spanish translation.

4. Practice

  1. Join with nonetheless: The weather was bad. We continued the trip. — Une con nonetheless: «Hacía mal tiempo. Continuamos el viaje».
  2. Is nonetheless generally more formal or less formal than but? — ¿Nonetheless suele ser más formal o menos formal que but?

Answers: The weather was bad. Nonetheless, we continued the trip. — Hacía mal tiempo. No obstante, continuamos el viaje. · More formal.
