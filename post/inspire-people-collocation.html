Inspire People: A Natural English Collocation

Learn inspire people and the useful pattern inspire someone to do something.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B1–B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

To inspire someone is to give them enthusiasm, confidence, ideas, or motivation. Inspire people is a natural collocation in leadership, education, creativity, and everyday English.

A very useful pattern is inspire + person + to + base verb: The teacher inspired me to study languages. — El profesor me inspiró a estudiar idiomas.

2. Bilingual examples

Her story inspired many people.
Su historia inspiró a muchas personas.
The teacher inspired me to study languages.
El profesor me inspiró a estudiar idiomas.
Great leaders inspire people to exceed their own expectations.
Los grandes líderes inspiran a las personas a superar sus propias expectativas.

3. Rules and common mistakes

In more formal English, inspire can combine with nouns such as confidence, hope, and loyalty. Adverbs such as deeply or truly can add emphasis when needed.

Do not confuse inspire with motivate in every context. They overlap, but inspire often suggests creating enthusiasm, ideas, or a sense of possibility.

Study tip: Read the English sentence aloud first, then check the Spanish meaning.

4. Practice

  1. Complete: His example inspired us ___ keep going. — Completa: «Su ejemplo nos inspiró a ___ adelante».
  2. Translate: Su creatividad inspira a la gente.

Answers: to · Her creativity inspires people. — Su creatividad inspira a la gente. — Su creatividad inspira a la gente.
