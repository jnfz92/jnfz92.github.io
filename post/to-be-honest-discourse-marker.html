To Be Honest: A Conversational Discourse Marker

Use to be honest naturally to introduce a candid opinion without overusing it.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B1–B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

To be honest introduces a frank or personal comment. It often signals that the speaker is about to say what they genuinely think or feel.

It commonly appears at the beginning followed by a comma, but it can also appear later: I don't like it, to be honest. — No me gusta, la verdad.

2. Bilingual examples

To be honest, I found the first module difficult.
Sinceramente, el primer módulo me pareció difícil.
To be honest, I'd rather stay home.
La verdad, preferiría quedarme en casa.
I don't like it, to be honest.
No me gusta, la verdad.

3. Rules and common mistakes

It is natural in speech and informal or semi-formal writing. In a formal report or academic essay, more neutral phrasing is usually preferable.

Avoid using it at the start of every answer. Repetition can sound mechanical and may accidentally imply that other statements are less honest.

Study tip: Say the English example aloud before checking the Spanish translation.

4. Practice

  1. Add punctuation: To be honest I expected a different result. — Añade la puntuación: «La verdad, esperaba un resultado diferente».
  2. Translate: La verdad, todavía no lo sé.

Answers: To be honest, I expected a different result. — La verdad, esperaba un resultado diferente. · To be honest, I still don't know. — La verdad, todavía no lo sé.
