Polite Disagreement: I Beg to Differ and Other Useful Phrases

Disagree clearly and respectfully in collaborative speaking and real conversations.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

Polite disagreement lets you challenge an idea while keeping the discussion collaborative. A useful pattern is acknowledgement + disagreement + reason.

I beg to differ is formal and somewhat traditional. Sorry to differ is polite and concise. Sorry, but I disagree is direct, clear, and natural.

2. Bilingual examples

I beg to differ; face-to-face interaction still has important advantages.
Permíteme discrepar; la interacción cara a cara sigue teniendo ventajas importantes.
Sorry, but I disagree with that point.
Lo siento, pero no estoy de acuerdo con ese punto.
I see your point, but I think there is another factor to consider.
Entiendo tu punto, pero creo que hay otro factor que considerar.

3. Rules and common mistakes

Useful softeners include I see your point, but…; That's true to some extent, but…; and I'm not sure I agree because….

Politeness does not mean hiding your position. State the disagreement clearly, then support it with a reason, example, or alternative.

Study tip: Say the English example aloud before checking the Spanish translation.

4. Practice

  1. Make this more polite: You're wrong. Online classes are better.
  2. Complete: I see your ___, but I'm not sure I agree.

Answers: Example: I see your point, but I think online classes have some important advantages. · point
