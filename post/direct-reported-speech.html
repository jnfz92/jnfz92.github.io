Direct Speech and Reported Speech

Learn how to report what someone said by changing tenses, pronouns, and time expressions.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

Direct speech repeats exact words in quotation marks. Reported speech communicates the message without quoting every word.

With a past reporting verb, English often backshifts: present simple → past simple; present continuous → past continuous; present perfect → past perfect; past simple → past perfect.

2. Bilingual examples

“I am tired,” she said. → She said that she was tired.
«Estoy cansada», dijo. → Dijo que estaba cansada.
“I have finished.” → He said he had finished.
«He terminado». → Dijo que había terminado.
“I haven't submitted the report yet,” he explained. → He explained that he hadn't submitted the report yet.
«Todavía no he entregado el informe», explicó. → Explicó que todavía no lo había entregado.

3. Rules and common mistakes

Pronouns and time/place expressions may change with the new viewpoint: tomorrow can become the next day, here can become there, and now can become then.

Backshift is not always compulsory when the information remains true. Meaning and context matter more than mechanical transformation.

Study tip: Read the English sentence aloud first. Try to explain it before checking the Spanish translation.

4. Practice

  1. Report: “I am leaving now,” Ana said.
  2. Report: “I saw him yesterday,” Luis said.

Answers: Ana said that she was leaving then. · Luis said that he had seen him the day before.
