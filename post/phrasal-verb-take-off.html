Take Off: Four Essential Meanings

Master take off for aircraft, clothing, sudden success, and time away from work.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B1–B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

Take off is a multi-meaning phrasal verb. With aircraft it means leave the ground; with clothing it means remove an item.

For success, a business, product, career, or trend can take off when it suddenly becomes popular or successful.

2. Bilingual examples

The flight is scheduled to take off at 8:30 a.m.
El vuelo está programado para despegar a las 8:30.
Please take off your coat.
Por favor, quítate el abrigo.
Her consultancy really took off last year.
Su consultoría despegó de verdad el año pasado.
He took two days off to recover.
Se tomó dos días libres para recuperarse.

3. Rules and common mistakes

Take time off means spend a period away from work or study. Notice the time expression between take and off: take two days off — tomarse dos días libres.

Context normally makes the meaning clear. With clothing, take off is separable: take off your coat / take your coat off — quitarte el abrigo.

Study tip: Read the English sentence aloud first, then check the Spanish meaning.

4. Practice

  1. Translate: Me voy a tomar el viernes libre.
  2. What does take off mean in: The new app took off in June? — ¿Qué significa take off en «La nueva aplicación despegó / tuvo mucho éxito en junio»?

Answers: I'm going to take Friday off. — Voy a tomarme el viernes libre. · It became successful/popular quickly. — Se hizo popular / tuvo éxito rápidamente.
