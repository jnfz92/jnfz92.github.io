Present Perfect: Never vs. Haven’t … Yet

Understand the difference between never and not … yet and avoid double negatives.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B1–B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

Both forms connect the past with the present, but they express different ideas. Never means “at no time up to now”; not … yet means “not until now” and often suggests an expected action.

Use have/has + never + past participle. Never already has a negative meaning, so do not add not: I have never been to New York.

2. Bilingual examples

I have never been to New York.
Nunca he estado en Nueva York.
I haven't received the confirmation email yet.
Todavía no he recibido el correo de confirmación.
Have you spoken to Marta yet?
¿Ya has hablado con Marta?

3. Rules and common mistakes

In negative present-perfect sentences, yet normally goes at the end: I haven't received the email yet. It is also common in questions: Have you finished yet? — ¿Ya has terminado?

Compare: I've never written a novel — Nunca he escrito una novela — describes life experience; I haven't finished my novel yet — Todavía no he terminado mi novela — describes an unfinished expected action.

Study tip: Read the English sentence aloud first. Try to explain it before checking the Spanish translation.

4. Practice

  1. Correct: I haven't never visited London.
  2. Complete: She ___ finished the assignment ___.

Answers: I have never visited London. · hasn't / yet
