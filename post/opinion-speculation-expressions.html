I Reckon, I Guess, I Believe, and It Seems Like

Compare four common ways to state opinions, deductions, and tentative impressions.

Detailed guide · Recommended level B2

In this article
  1. Meaning and use
  2. Bilingual examples
  3. Rules and common mistakes
  4. Practice

1. Meaning and use

Hedging makes an opinion less absolute. It helps you show whether you are giving a personal view, a tentative deduction, or an impression based on evidence.

I reckon is informal and especially associated with British and Australian English. I guess is widespread and often tentative. I believe is neutral to slightly formal. It seems like focuses on an impression.

2. Bilingual examples

I reckon we should finish this task first.
Creo que deberíamos terminar esta tarea primero.
I guess they changed the date because of the weather.
Supongo que cambiaron la fecha por el tiempo.
I believe continuous learning is important.
Creo que el aprendizaje continuo es importante.
It seems like the update fixed the problem.
Parece que la actualización solucionó el problema.

3. Rules and common mistakes

Choose the expression according to register and meaning, not simply as four interchangeable versions of I think.

For formal writing, I believe or it seems that may fit better than I reckon. In natural conversation, I guess and it seems like are extremely useful.

Study tip: Say the English example aloud before checking the Spanish translation.

4. Practice

  1. Which expression is especially informal British/Australian?
  2. Translate: Parece que están ocupados.

Answers: I reckon. — Yo diría / creo. · It seems like they're busy. / It seems that they're busy. — Parece que están ocupados.
