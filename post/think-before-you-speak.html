Think Before You Speak

A practical English guide to pausing, choosing your words carefully, and expressing yourself with more clarity and respect.

Reflection + useful English · Recommended level A2–B1

In this article

  2. Useful phrases to give yourself time

1. Why thinking first matters

Speaking quickly is not always the same as speaking well. In a conversation, a short pause can help you understand what you really want to say, choose a better tone, and avoid words you may regret later.

In English, you do not need to answer immediately. Native and fluent speakers often use short expressions to create thinking time. These phrases can make you sound calmer and more natural, not less confident.

Main idea: A pause is not an empty space. It can be a moment to choose a clearer, kinder and more useful response.

2. Useful phrases to give yourself time

These expressions are useful when you need a few seconds before answering.

Let me think for a moment.
Déjame pensar un momento.
That's a good question.
Es una buena pregunta.
I'm not sure how to put it.
No estoy seguro/a de cómo expresarlo.
What I mean is...
Lo que quiero decir es...
Let me rephrase that.
Déjame decirlo de otra manera.
I need a second to think about that.
Necesito un segundo para pensar en eso.

Notice that these phrases do two things: they give you time and they tell the other person what is happening. That makes the pause feel natural.

3. A simple three-question filter

Before saying something important, ask yourself three quick questions:

1. Is it true?
Do I know this, or am I assuming it?
2. Is it useful?
Will saying this help the conversation move forward?
3. Can I say it better?
Can I express the same idea more clearly or respectfully?

This does not mean avoiding disagreement. You can be direct without being careless.

You're wrong.
Estás equivocado/a.		I see it differently.

I don't care.

4. Common communication mistakes

Answering before you understand. If the question is unclear, ask for clarification first.

Could you clarify what you mean?
¿Podrías aclarar qué quieres decir?

Using absolute words too quickly. Words such as always and never can make a disagreement sound stronger than you intend.

You never listen.
Nunca escuchas.
I don't feel heard in this conversation.
No siento que se me esté escuchando en esta conversación.

Confusing honesty with harshness. Being honest does not require the strongest possible wording. Clear language is often more effective when it focuses on the idea or situation rather than attacking the person.

Study tip: When practising speaking, deliberately wait two seconds before answering. Use one thinking phrase, then give your response.

5. Practice

  1. Translate into English: “Déjame pensar un momento.”
  2. Make this sentence more thoughtful: “You're wrong.”
  3. Complete: “I'm not sure how to ___ it.”
  4. Which question helps you check your tone: “Is it true?”, “Is it useful?” or “Can I say it better?”
